Right now, your phone has an address, and so does every website you visit. It's called an IP address, and it works just like the street address of your house. When you open a site, your device sends a small packet labeled with your address and the destination. Routers along the way read that label and pass the packet forward, hop by hop, until it arrives. Then the website replies to your address, and the page appears in about a tenth of a second. The catch: websites only see one shared address for your whole home, and it can change.
