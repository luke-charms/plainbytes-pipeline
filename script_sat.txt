Your internet can come from space, so how does it get all the way down here? Picture a satellite as a giant cell tower floating above you. Your dish sends a signal up to the satellite, which beams it down to a ground station wired to the internet. Old satellites sat extremely high, so every message took a very long trip. Newer ones orbit much lower and move fast, so thousands of them work together and hand you off like a relay race. The catch: a dish needs a clear view of the sky, so trees and roofs can block it.
