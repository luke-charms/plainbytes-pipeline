A QR code is just a picture, so how does your phone read it? Think of it as a tiny map made of black and white squares. Each square is a one or a zero, and together they spell out a web address. Three big squares in the corners tell your camera which way is up, so you can scan from any angle. Your phone reads the squares in order, turns them into text, and opens the link. The clever part: QR codes carry extra backup data, so even a scratched code still works. The catch: a QR code can hide any link, so check the address before you tap. Next up: how Wi-Fi works.
