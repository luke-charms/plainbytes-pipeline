Your data hops across many machines online, and none of them can read it. Think of mailing a letter in a locked box that only you and the website can open. First, the website shows an ID, called a certificate, so your browser knows it's the real one. Then your browser and the site use clever math to agree on a secret key, without ever sending it. From then on, everything is scrambled with that key, so spies only see gibberish. The catch: HTTPS hides what you send, not always which site you visit, and a fake site can have a padlock too.
