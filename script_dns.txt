Your phone can't read website names, and it never could. So how does it find them? Think of D N S as the phone book of the internet. You type a name, like pizza dot com. Your phone asks a D N S server, and it looks up the matching number, called an IP address. Then your phone calls that number directly, and the page loads. Servers remember answers for a while, so the next lookup is almost instant. The catch: if D N S breaks, the whole internet seems down, even though the websites are fine. Next up: how cookies work.
