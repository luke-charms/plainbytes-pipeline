Quantum computers aren't just faster computers, and their biggest problem is that they make mistakes. Think of a coin spinning in the air: until it lands, it's neither heads nor tails. A qubit works like that, holding a blend of zero and one while it calculates. Combining those blends cleverly lets quantum computers solve certain problems far faster. The catch: the slightest heat, vibration, or stray signal knocks the coin out of the air, and the answer is ruined. So engineers spread one piece of information across many qubits, and keep checking them for errors. Even then, new studies say those errors are more complicated than expected.
