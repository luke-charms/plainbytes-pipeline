Your phone never tells satellites where you are, so how does GPS know? Think of counting the seconds between lightning and thunder. Each GPS satellite just broadcasts its position and the exact time, over and over. Your phone listens, and measures how long each signal took, because radio travels at the speed of light. Three satellites narrow that down to one spot, and a fourth fixes your phone's clock. The catch: those signals are faint, so jammers can drown them out. Next up: how Wi-Fi works.
