AI doesn't run on your computer's main brain. It runs on a different chip: the GPU. A CPU is like one genius chef, cooking one dish at a time, very fast. A GPU is a kitchen with thousands of cooks, each doing one simple step at once. Drawing a game screen and training an AI both turn into millions of tiny math problems that don't depend on each other. So instead of solving them one by one, a GPU solves thousands together. That's why companies are buying them by the hundreds of thousands, and why data centers full of them use so much power. The catch: a GPU is bad at everything else, so your CPU still runs the show. Next up: how drones deliver packages. Follow PlainBytes for more tech, explained simply.
